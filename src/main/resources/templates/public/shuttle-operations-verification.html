Direct answer

How does RoutePulse verify employee shuttle service?

RoutePulse compares the planned assignment with evidence created during the operating day. That includes authenticated driver sessions, QR or NFC checkpoint activity, schedule context, rider confirmations, live operational status, and management review. The result is a service record that distinguishes a plan from actual execution.

Actual operations are more complicated than “started” and “ended.”

QR and NFC checkpoints

Drivers can use the same checkpoint workflow through QR or NFC, reducing the number of separate operational steps they need to remember.

Pause and resume

One scheduled assignment can contain multiple actual sessions when service stops for a break, vehicle issue, mistaken ending, or another real-world interruption.

Driver replacement

When a different driver takes over, actual activity changes without rewriting the scheduled-driver history.

Rider-side confirmation

Rider confirmations can provide an additional operational signal for management review rather than relying on one source of evidence alone.

Verification is not the same as continuous GPS fleet tracking

RoutePulse's current verification model is centered on the operating workflow: authenticated checkpoints, driving sessions, workforce schedule context, rider signals, and manager review. It is designed to answer operational questions such as who was scheduled, who actually drove, when service was active, and where the plan changed.

That makes RoutePulse useful even when the organization already has a separate vehicle or fleet system and needs a stronger record of the employee-shuttle process itself.

Shuttle service verification FAQs

How does RoutePulse verify that somebody drove?

RoutePulse records authenticated driving sessions and can compare them with the planned assignment, schedule context, QR/NFC activity, rider confirmations, and management review signals.

Can a driver pause and resume?

Yes. RoutePulse supports multiple actual sessions under one scheduled assignment, which allows service to stop and resume without pretending it was one uninterrupted block.

What if the actual driver was not the scheduled driver?

The scheduled driver remains part of the plan, while the actual session records the person who drove. This keeps accountability and workload reporting clearer.

Does RoutePulse continuously track a vehicle by GPS?

The current product is focused on operational verification rather than continuous GPS telematics. Its evidence comes from driver checkpoints, driving sessions, schedule context, rider signals, and manager review.
